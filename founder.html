The founder — Nairobi / Melbourne

Built by the person who does the work.

FINAKI was founded by Imran Shiundu. Engineer first, designer by obsession — this is the founder's story in his own words: why the studio exists, and why one person doing strategy, design and code beats a handoff chain.

01

Who I am

( The person )

My name is Imran Shiundu. I founded FINAKI, and I'm the person in every email, every design file and every commit that leaves this studio. No account managers, no handoffs — the person who listens to your story is the person who designs the proof and ships the code.

I come to design from engineering. That changes what FINAKI is: the sites we make aren't just art-directed, they're engineered — fast, accessible, and built to be maintained, not just launched.

Imran Shiundu Founder, FINAKI
02

Why I started it

( The gap )

FINAKI started with one observation I couldn't shake: the most ambitious founders were being undersold by their own websites. Real businesses, real traction — and a presence that looked like a template.

Not because they lacked vision — because nobody building their presence ever asked for the story. I do the opposite. Every project starts with your story, not my portfolio. I dig until I find what makes you irreplaceable, then I design until the internet agrees.

That's the whole thesis: great founders changing the world deserve a presence as powerful as what they're building. Everything FINAKI does — strategy, identity, websites — exists to close that gap.

04

Beyond the studio

( The engineering spine )

FINAKI isn't the only thing I've founded — and that's the point. I'm also the founder and lead engineer of TapTap Africa, a Nairobi cashless payments platform, and I teach full-stack programming at kood/Nairobi, the Nairobi campus of an Estonian programming school. My full work lives at www.imranisdev.top.

That engineering spine is why FINAKI sites behave the way they do. Design that only has to survive a portfolio shot is easy. Design that has to survive real users, real traffic and real money is a different discipline — and it's the one I practice daily.
